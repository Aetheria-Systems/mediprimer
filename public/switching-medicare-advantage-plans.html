Switching Medicare Advantage Plans During Open Enrollment: What to Check First

Moving from one Medicare Advantage plan to another is the most common Open Enrollment move. Here's what to confirm before you do it.

Here's the short answer. To switch from one Medicare Advantage plan to another, just enroll in the new plan during Open Enrollment, October 15 through December 7, 2026 — your old plan ends automatically when the new one starts on January 1. Before you enroll, check five things: your drugs, your doctors, the total cost, whether you're leaving employer coverage behind, and whether your current plan is even still being offered.

You don't need a reason to switch. You can join, drop, or switch to another Medicare Advantage plan for any reason during this window, with or without drug coverage. You also don't need to cancel your old plan — it ends on its own once the new one begins.

Check 1: Read your Annual Notice of Change first

Before deciding you need to switch, read the Annual Notice of Change your current plan mailed by September 30. It tells you exactly what's different for 2027: premium, deductible, drug list, and network. If nothing important changed, switching may not be worth the effort. If your costs rose, a drug was dropped, or your doctor left the network, that's your reason to compare.

Check 2: Your specific drugs, on the new plan's formulary

A different Medicare Advantage plan can have a completely different drug list, called a formulary, even if both plans look similar on the surface. Enter every prescription you take, with the dose, into Medicare Plan Finder before you switch. Check which tier each drug lands on and whether the new plan requires prior authorization or step therapy — rules your current plan might not have. See our Plan Finder walkthrough and how to check a formulary before switching.

Check 3: Your doctors and hospitals, in the new plan's network

Medicare Advantage plans build their own provider networks, and a doctor in-network with your current plan may not be in-network with the one you're considering. Call each doctor's office directly and ask whether they're in the new plan's network and accepting new patients — Plan Finder's directory isn't always current.

Check 4: The total estimated yearly cost, not just the premium

Compare the total estimated cost Plan Finder calculates for each plan — premium, deductible, copays, and coinsurance added up for a year based on the drugs and pharmacy you enter. A lower premium on the new plan doesn't guarantee a lower total cost. See How to Compare Medicare Plans for 2027 for the full comparison order.

Check 5: Whether you'd be leaving other coverage behind

If you also have employer, union, or retiree coverage, talk to that benefits office before switching Medicare Advantage plans. Joining a different Medicare Advantage plan can cause you to lose that other coverage, and you may not be able to get it back.

If your current plan is leaving Medicare

Sometimes the "switch" isn't optional: an insurer can decide each year whether to keep offering a plan in your county. If your plan is leaving, you need to join another Medicare Advantage plan or return to Original Medicare. You can do this during Open Enrollment, and if your plan's contract with Medicare isn't renewed you also get an extra window from December 8 through the last day of February. If you don't pick a new Medicare Advantage plan, you default into Original Medicare.

Switching to Original Medicare instead

If you're considering leaving Medicare Advantage entirely rather than moving to another Advantage plan, the process and the trade-offs are different — particularly around buying a Medigap policy afterward. See our separate guide: Switching from Medicare Advantage to Original Medicare.

How to actually make the switch

  1. Compare plans using Medicare Plan Finder with your drugs and pharmacy entered.
  2. Confirm your doctors are in-network with the new plan by calling their offices directly.
  3. Enroll in the new plan online through Plan Finder, by contacting the plan directly, by mailing a form the plan must receive by December 7, or by calling 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227, TTY 1-877-486-2048).
  4. Don't cancel your old plan. It ends automatically when your new coverage starts January 1.

Free, unbiased help is available from your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP), which isn't connected to any insurance company or plan.

Common questions

Do I need to cancel my old Medicare Advantage plan before switching?

No. When you enroll in a new Medicare Advantage plan, your old plan ends automatically. You don't need to notify or cancel it yourself.

When does a plan switch made during Open Enrollment take effect?

January 1 of the following year, as long as the new plan receives your request by December 7.

Can I switch Medicare Advantage plans again after December 7 if I change my mind?

Yes, once. If you're already in a Medicare Advantage plan, the Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period from January 1 to March 31 lets you switch to another Medicare Advantage plan or return to Original Medicare and join a drug plan.
