What's Changing in Medicare Costs for 2027

Premiums, deductibles, and drug-cost limits, and where to find the confirmed 2027 numbers once Medicare publishes them.

Here's the short answer. As of this page's last update, Medicare had not yet published the 2027 Part B premium and deductible or the 2027 Part D deductible cap and out-of-pocket limit. CMS typically announces these figures in the fall, and they show up in your Annual Notice of Change, the "Medicare & You" handbook, and on Medicare.gov. This page explains which costs change every year, what the 2026 baseline is, and exactly where to look once the 2027 numbers are out.

The standard figures on one printable page: Medicare cost card.

Don't wait on the 2027 numbers to start comparing plans. Your plan's premium, deductible, and drug tiers for 2027 arrive in its Annual Notice of Change by September 30, regardless of when CMS finalizes the government-wide Part A/B figures. See How to Compare Medicare Plans for 2027.

What changes every January, and why

Four kinds of Medicare costs are recalculated every year:

  • Part A premium and deductible — most people pay no Part A premium, but the inpatient hospital deductible is set annually.
  • Part B premium and deductible — the standard monthly premium and yearly deductible, plus the income brackets for IRMAA surcharges.
  • Part D deductible cap and out-of-pocket limit — the maximum deductible any drug plan may charge, and the yearly out-of-pocket spending level after which catastrophic coverage begins.
  • Your own plan's premium, drug list, and network — set by your specific Medicare Advantage or Part D plan, not by CMS, and can change independently of the government-wide figures.

Where the 2027 numbers will appear, and when

  1. Your plan's Annual Notice of Change (ANOC). Mailed by September 30, 2026, this letter gives your specific 2027 premium, deductible, and drug tiers before the government-wide figures are even finalized. See reading your ANOC.
  2. The new "Medicare & You" handbook. Mailed to every Medicare household in late September.
  3. Medicare.gov's costs page. Updated once CMS finalizes the Part A and Part B figures, typically announced in the fall.
  4. MediPrimer's Understanding Your Costs page. We update it as soon as the official 2027 figures are published.

How this affects your Open Enrollment decision

You don't need the final 2027 Part A/B numbers to compare plans during Open Enrollment (October 15 – December 7, 2026). Your plan's own 2027 costs, in its ANOC, are what actually determines what you'll pay. Use Medicare Plan Finder to compare plans using those plan-specific numbers, and check how to compare plans for 2027 for the four things worth checking, in order.

Common questions

Has Medicare announced the 2027 Part B premium yet?

Not as of this page's last check. CMS typically announces the coming year's Part B premium and deductible in the fall, usually around November, after this page's dateModified. Check Medicare.gov or your "Medicare & You" handbook for the confirmed 2027 number.

Why does my premium or drug cost change every January?

Medicare updates Part A and Part B premiums and deductibles, and the Part D deductible cap and out-of-pocket limit, every year. Medicare Advantage and Part D plans can also change their own premiums, drug lists, and networks each January.

Where will I see my plan's actual 2027 costs?

In your plan's Annual Notice of Change, mailed by September 30, and in the Evidence of Coverage that follows by October 15.
