Medicaid Retroactive Coverage Is Shrinking in 2027

Medicaid can cover bills from before the month you applied. A new federal law shortens that look-back for applications made on or after January 1, 2027.

Here's the short answer. Today, Medicaid must cover you for up to 3 months before the month you applied, if you got covered care in that time and would have qualified. For applications made on or after January 1, 2027, that drops. Adults in the Medicaid "adult group" (the expansion group) get 1 month. Everyone else gets 2 months. The date that counts is when you apply, not when you got care.

What retroactive coverage is

Retroactive coverage lets Medicaid pay for care you got before you applied. This helps when someone has a hospital stay first and applies later. Under current federal rules, states must give applicants this look-back for up to three months before the month of application. The person must have received Medicaid-covered services and must have been eligible at the time.

What changes on January 1, 2027

Section 71112 of Public Law 119-21 (a 2025 law) shortens the look-back. The change is for applications made on or after January 1, 2027.

Months of retroactive Medicaid coverage before the month you apply
Who you areApplications before Jan 1, 2027Applications on or after Jan 1, 2027
Adults in the Medicaid adult group (expansion adults)Up to 3 months1 month
Everyone else in MedicaidUp to 3 months2 months

The adult group is, roughly, adults under 65 who qualify by income alone. It does not include people who qualify through disability, age, or pregnancy. For separate CHIP programs, retroactive coverage is optional for states. For applications made on or after January 1, 2027, a state that offers it cannot start coverage earlier than two months before the month of application.

An example (for illustration only)

Maria is an adult in the expansion group. She goes to the hospital in March 2027. She applies for Medicaid in April 2027. Retroactive coverage can reach back one month before April. That is March, so the March stay can be covered if she was eligible then. Under the old 3-month rule, it could have reached back to January. If her hospital care had been in February, it would fall outside the new look-back. This example uses the month-before-application rule in the CMS letter. Your state's exact start date can differ.

The lesson is simple: apply fast. The sooner you apply, the fewer bills fall outside the window.

What to do after a hospital stay or sudden illness

  • Apply right away. Do not wait for the bills to pile up. Medicaid takes applications all year.
  • Ask the hospital for help. Many hospitals have financial counselors who help with Medicaid applications.
  • Keep every bill and notice. You may need them to show what care you got and when.
  • Ask your state about its start date. Coverage can begin on the application date or the first day of the application month, depending on the state plan.

Not sure how to apply? See Starting Out with Medicaid and Medicaid Eligibility. Find your state's agency in the State Medicaid directory. If you lost coverage, see Lost Medicaid: What Now?

Why this matters most for some households

People who wait to apply are hit hardest. This can include families who are unsure whether they qualify, or who fear that applying will cause problems. If you are unsure, ask a trusted source such as your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) or a hospital financial counselor. Rules on immigration status are also changing. See Immigrant Eligibility Changes. The same 2025 law also adds work requirements and 6-month renewals for expansion adults. See Medicaid Renewal for how yearly renewals work today.

Common Questions

Is Medicaid retroactive coverage ending?

No. It is getting shorter. For applications made on or after January 1, 2027, the look-back is 1 month for expansion adults and 2 months for everyone else in Medicaid.

Does the new rule apply if I apply in 2026?

No. The shorter look-back applies to applications made on or after January 1, 2027. CMS has said it expects to issue more guidance, so check with your state.

Does Medicaid always cover the whole look-back?

No. You must have received Medicaid-covered services in that time, and you must have been eligible then.

Where do I apply?

Apply through your state Medicaid agency. Our State Medicaid directory links to each one.

Verify at the source. States are still setting up this change. Details come from your state. Read the federal summary in the CMS Informational Bulletin (Nov. 18, 2025) and ask your state Medicaid agency how it applies to you.
