Medicaid Renewals Every 6 Months Starting in 2027

Expansion adults will renew twice a year instead of once. Here is who is affected, when it starts, and how to stay covered.

Here's the short answer. If you have Medicaid as an adult through the expansion (the "adult group"), your state must, in almost all cases, check your eligibility every 6 months instead of every 12. This starts with renewals scheduled on or after January 1, 2027. You still keep your coverage if you still qualify. The risk is missing a notice. With two renewals a year, there are twice as many chances to miss one.

Who renews every 6 months

The rule covers almost everyone in the Medicaid adult group. That is, roughly, adults who qualify by income alone. It also covers people in this group who are covered through a state waiver that gives full coverage to the whole group.

It does not change the schedule for:

  • Certain American Indians and Alaska Natives in the adult group.
  • People in other income-based groups, such as children.
  • People in groups based on age, disability, or other factors that do not use income rules alone.

These people keep renewing at least every 12 months.

When you switch to the 6-month schedule

If you apply and your coverage starts on or after January 1, 2027, your state must give you a 6-month eligibility period.

If you already have Medicaid on January 1, 2027, your state picks one of two ways to move you.

  • Option 1: The state moves your renewal earlier, to get as close to a 6-month period as it can. It cannot move it earlier than January 1, 2027.
  • Option 2: The state keeps your current renewal date. At that first renewal in 2027, you get a 6-month period.

Which option your state uses decides when you first see the new schedule. Check your state's notices. Whatever the schedule, your state must also re-check you between renewals if something changes that could affect eligibility.

An example from CMS

CMS gives this sample. A person applies on June 5, 2027. Their state starts coverage on the first day of the month of application. Their eligibility period runs from June 1 through November 30, 2027. The state starts the renewal about 90 days before the period ends. In CMS's example, that is September 1. If the person still qualifies, the next 6-month period runs December 1, 2027 through May 31, 2028.

What the renewal process looks like

The rule changes how often, not how. The steps stay the same:

  1. The state checks its data first. It tries to renew you without contacting you, using reliable data it already has.
  2. If it cannot, it sends you a pre-filled form. You get at least 30 days to return the form and any papers it asks for.
  3. Before ending your coverage, the state must look at all ways you might qualify. It must give at least 10 days' notice and tell you about your right to a fair hearing.

How to stay covered

  • Keep your address, phone, and email current with your state Medicaid agency.
  • Open every letter from Medicaid. Write down the due date.
  • Return the form on time, even if you think nothing changed.
  • Ask for help early. Your SHIP or a local navigator can help.
  • If coverage ends, act fast. See Lost Medicaid: What Now?

Work requirements add to the paperwork

Many people on 6-month renewals must also show "community engagement" (work or similar activity) starting January 1, 2027. Your state will check this at each renewal. But almost everyone in the adult group renews every 6 months, whether or not the work rule applies to them. See Medicaid Work Requirements for who is exempt. Another change from the same law shortens retroactive coverage. For how renewal works now, read Medicaid Renewal.

Common Questions

Do I have to renew Medicaid every 6 months?

If you are an expansion adult, almost always yes, starting with renewals scheduled on or after January 1, 2027. Most other groups keep the 12-month schedule.

Will I lose coverage because of this change?

Not if you still qualify and you respond to your state's notices. The main risk is a missed notice or late form.

When is my next renewal?

Your state's notice has the date. If you are not sure, contact your state Medicaid agency.

Does this affect Medicare?

No. Medicare has its own rules. If you have both Medicare and Medicaid, see the dual-eligible guide, since your Medicaid renewal still follows your state's schedule.
